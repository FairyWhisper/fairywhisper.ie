Curriculum Extracurricular

November 17, 2026

I started with some activities, but then I needed a map

It has been two weeks of almost constant tests and exams in my hDip "life" and honestly, I've felt so disconnected from learning and producing.

For anyone who knows me, or has ever met me, you will know of my disdain for jargon, buzzwords, and unnecessarily complicated language. It's served me surprisingly well, even in formal academic and scientific settings. So several excruciating multiple choice quizzes testing me on how many TLAs (three letter acronyms) I can reel off has basically fried my brain.

I still have tests to do, but change is as good as a rest or so I've heard, which brought me back to the Code Club - Fairymount Code&Create.

The idea was so simple...

  • Have kids (done)
  • Do science (quite regular in our house)
  • Add more kids
  • Add more science
  • Add in the entire teaching philosophy and pedagogy of the Raspberry Pi Foundation (surprisingly, done!)
  • Make activity pages and resources for the Code Club (work in progress, thoroughly enjoyable work in progress)

But wait...

  • I should align this with the new primary school STE+M curriculum
  • I should read the entire Primary Curriculum to be fully informed
  • I should trawl the internet for relevant and appropriate resources to use for the activity sheets

Why is there so much?!

I need a map

If I need a map, and I'm organising a volunteer code club, then I reckon there's an overworked teacher trying to integrate the new curriculum into an already busy schedule, while juggling the million other things they do, who might also benefit from this map...

A handbook has spawned

I think there's always a point in anything FairyWhisper where there's a chance I've gone too big. Honestly in this case, I think I've gone exactly where I've needed to go. There are so many amazing resources, printables, and cheatsheets available online. Really cool, interesting activities to do with kids of all ages. How do you decide on one though? Or a series of activities to follow?

I'm a mam, I'm busy, yes, but I'm doing this as a hobby, as an interest, I give the time and effort to this task because I have it to give and I enjoy doing it. Honestly, my awe of primary teachers has increased exponentially over the past few weeks. The curriculum is huge, but the implementation guidelines read more like "Children will learn things in an unspecified manner. Perhaps make a podcast." than concrete support for a busy classroom teacher.

But I digress, back to the handbook, the mapping, the possible madness...

I started out with a vague plan to convert some of the activities for Code&Create into classroom-friendly formats. It was simple, straight forward. Just a little document to hand to my kid's school and maybe save the teachers some time trawling through the extensive momosphere, homeschooler blogs, education resources that can't decide if they're for teacher or infant perusal.

I started the document, super simple, right? A few headings, a couple of activities, written in scientific format, not so children can "play" scientist, so they can be scientists, doing science.

Choice paralysis and cognitive overload were a very real deciding factor here, though. There really are so many things you can do. What should be done? what should be covered? What is missing from the most common "kid science" activities? Where are the gaps?

Now, I don't know about you, but there's nothing I love more than a bit of metadata to soothe my weary soul of an evening. You know, it's a perfectly normal and sane hobby.

And so it began... "the handbook"; now "STEAM Powered Activities; Integrated Science, Technology, Engineering, Arts and Mathematics Activities for Primary Schools" (title's a bit wordy, granted)

The activities aren't novel, unique, or groundbreaking. The only thing I've done is collate them and write them up in lab manual format. And add the curriculum links. And backlink through the curriculum strands and strand units. Key competencies too. Now I'm curating the activity list to provide a broad coverage of the entire STEM curriculum (and its relevant integrations) to make a searchable, usable guide for teachers, so they can do the fun part, the beneficial part, and the planning / reporting / paperwork part without adding a million more hours to their work week.

Honestly, I feel like "outsource your organisational paperwork and architecture to me, please" is my calling in life!

And on that note, F&T have outsourced the organisation and planning of getting snacks to me so I should probably do that!
